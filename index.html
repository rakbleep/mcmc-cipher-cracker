How the Algorithm Works

The Brute Force Problem

Monoalphabetic substitution ciphers map each of the 26 letters to another letter. The total number of possible keys is 26! ≈ 4.03 × 1026. Testing 1 billion keys per second would still take 12 billion years—longer than the age of the Earth.

Metropolis–Hastings Random Walk

Instead of searching blindly, we define a probability landscape where English-like texts have exponentially higher probability. We propose swapping two letters at random. If the new key scores higher, we always accept. If lower, we accept with probability eΔscore to avoid getting trapped in local optima.

War & Peace Bigram Matrix

The scoring engine uses a 27×27 transition matrix M(x, y) precomputed from Leo Tolstoy's War and Peace (over 3 million characters). High-probability letter transitions like TH, HE, and IN quickly pull the Markov Chain straight toward intelligible plaintext.

Plausibility Function:   LogScore(f) = ∑i=1N-1 log M(f(ci), f(ci+1))
Acceptance Probability:   α(f → f') = min(1,   exp(Δ LogScore / T))
